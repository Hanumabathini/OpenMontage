## 15. Outlier and momentum analysis

Collect available video-level data: title, URL, channel, upload date/time, duration, public views, likes, comments, subscriber count, thumbnail, topic, format, language, market signals, source and collection timestamp. Record chart history only where actually available.

Calculate:

```text
Average lifetime views/hour = cumulative views / hours since publication
Recent view velocity = (views at t2 - views at t1) / hours between snapshots
View acceleration = change in recent view velocity across comparable time windows
Outlier multiplier = video views / median views of comparable age-matched channel uploads
```

Use at least 20 comparable uploads when available. Split long-form and Shorts; use age-matched measurements where possible. If only current lifetime views exist, label the baseline provisional and do not claim measured acceleration. If fewer than 10 comparable videos are available, mark baseline confidence low. Do not interpret different channel sizes, formats or accumulation windows as directly comparable without normalization.

| Outlier multiplier | Classification |
|---:|---|
| Below 1.5× | Normal or below baseline |
| 1.5× to below 2× | Mild outlier |
| 2× to below 5× | Strong outlier |
| 5× to 10× | Exceptional outlier |
| Above 10× | Major breakout candidate |

Prioritize strong relative outliers and fresh momentum over absolute views alone. Maintain repeated snapshots to evaluate sustained growth.
